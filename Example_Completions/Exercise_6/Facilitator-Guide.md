## Project Complexity Factors

This RFP includes multiple complexity factors that make bid evaluation challenging:

1. **Occupied building** with temperature-sensitive pharmaceutical tenant
2. **Phasing required** - maximum 15,000 sq ft exposed at once
3. **Three roof levels** (main roof, office wing, mezzanine)
4. **Mixed deck types** (steel + wood in office wing - condition unknown)
5. **47 penetrations** to detail around
6. **Known ponding** affecting 1,200 sq ft (must be addressed)
7. **15-20% wet insulation** (exact extent unknown)
8. **Potential hazardous materials** (building is 1998 - transition period)
9. **90-day schedule** with liquidated damages ($500/day)
10. **Multiple exclusions and allowances** across bids make price comparison difficult

## Detailed Bid Analysis

### Bid #1 - Lakeside Roofing Inc. ($487,500)

**Critical Red Flags:**
- **45 mil membrane** specified instead of required 60 mil (25% thinner) - NON-COMPLIANT
- **Single-layer insulation** instead of required two-layer with staggered joints - NON-COMPLIANT
- **15-year warranty** instead of required 20-year minimum - NON-COMPLIANT
- Cover board not mentioned - likely excluded
- Phasing plan is vague: "We will complete the roof in sections"
- States: "our inspection did not confirm significant ponding" - contradicts RFP
- 14 exclusions including tapered insulation, temperature monitoring, weather protection

**What They Missed:**
- Did not address ponding correction requirement
- No temperature monitoring protocol for pharmaceutical tenant
- Minimal phasing detail for occupied building
- Did not count penetrations accurately

**Possible Interpretation:**
This bidder may have:
- Intentionally under-specified to achieve low price
- Not thoroughly read the RFP specifications
- Insufficient experience with complex roof replacements
- Planning to change order for compliance items

**Discussion Point:** This bid appears lowest but has multiple non-compliances. Is this a honest mistake or intentional lowball? Either way, significant costs would be required to bring into compliance.

---

### Bid #2 - Summit Contracting Ltd. ($742,000)

**Strengths:**
- Exceeds all specifications (80 mil vs. 60 mil, R-38 vs. R-30)
- 30-year NDL warranty (best in class)
- 5-year workmanship warranty (double industry standard)
- Most comprehensive phasing plan (8 pages, dedicated phasing coordinator)
- Temperature monitoring included ($6,500 line item)
- Tapered insulation for ponding included ($28,000)
- Contingency allowance included ($15,000)
- Four references including pharmaceutical facility (Pfizer)
- COR certified, excellent WSIB rating (0.72)
- 25 years experience

**Considerations:**
- 59% higher than lowest bid
- May be "over-building" for this property
- Includes many items as standard that others exclude
- Best choice for long-term hold strategy

**What's Included That Others Exclude:**
- Temperature monitoring during construction
- Tapered insulation system
- Contingency for unknown conditions
- Dedicated phasing coordinator
- 10-year ponding warranty

**Discussion Point:** This is the quality/safety choice but at a significant premium. Is the 59% premium justified for an occupied pharmaceutical facility?

### Bid #4 - Metro Building Solutions Inc. ($468,000)

**Critical Red Flags:**
- **25 exclusions** - highest of all bidders
- **Cover board excluded** - required by RFP ("Not included...typically not required for industrial buildings" - contradicts RFP)
- **Tapered insulation excluded** - RFP requires ponding correction ("Ponding does not require correction unless causing active leaks" - contradicts RFP)
- **Phasing coordination excluded** - RFP requires phasing ("If phasing extends schedule beyond 90 days: $2,500/week")
- **Temperature monitoring excluded** - critical for pharmaceutical tenant
- **Weekend weather protection charged extra** - RFP requires daily watertight condition
- Only 1-year workmanship warranty (worst of all bidders)
- WSIB experience rating 1.12 (above 1.0 = worse than average safety record)
- Requires 20% upfront (vs. 10% standard)
- Material price escalation clause
- States skylights are 4 units (RFP says 12, RFP is correct)

**Potential Change Order Exposure:**
| Item | Estimated Cost |
|------|----------------|
| Cover board (required) | $38,000 |
| Tapered insulation (required) | $28,000 |
| Steel deck repairs | $25,000-$45,000 |
| Wood deck repairs | $15,000-$25,000 |
| Drains (6 estimated) | $14,400 |
| HVAC curbs (4 estimated) | $12,800 |
| Phasing overage (4 weeks estimated) | $10,000 |
| Weekend weather protection (18 weekends) | $27,000 |
| Permits | $4,500 |
| **Total potential add-ons** | **$174,700-$204,700** |
| **Adjusted bid** | **$642,700-$672,700** |

**Discussion Point:** This is the classic "lowball and change order" strategy. The base bid looks attractive but actual cost will likely exceed all other bidders. The exclusions list reads like a change order menu.

- Allowances for unknown conditions
- Phasing coordination

**Discussion Point:** This bidder demonstrates expertise by offering thoughtful alternatives with clear trade-offs. The VE analysis shows they understand both the technical and financial dimensions. Base bid is competitive, and VE options provide flexibility.

## Expected Participant Outcomes

### Using /vendor-review Command

Participants should run the Operations plugin's `/vendor-review` command on the bid package. The command should:

1. **Compare all 5 bids** against RFP requirements
2. **Identify compliance issues** (e.g., Lakeside's 45 mil membrane, Metro's exclusions)
3. **Flag risk factors** (e.g., Metro's 25 exclusions, safety ratings, phasing capabilities)
4. **Score each bidder** on price, quality, risk, and qualifications
5. **Provide recommendation** with rationale

### Discussion Questions

1. **Which bid represents the best value?** (Likely Pinnacle or Heritage)
2. **Which bid has the most hidden risk?** (Metro with 25+ exclusions)
3. **Which bids are non-compliant?** (Lakeside - multiple; Metro - several)
4. **Is the premium bid justified?** (Depends on hold period and risk tolerance)
5. **What would you negotiate with the selected bidder?**
6. **What references would you check?** (Especially pharmaceutical/occupied building references)
7. **How do you compare bids with different exclusions and allowances?**

### Key Learning Points

1. **Lowest bid ≠ best value** - Metro and Lakeside have major issues that would cost more in change orders
2. **Read exclusions carefully** - They become change orders (Metro has 25+)
3. **Check compliance first** - Lakeside is non-compliant on membrane, insulation, and warranty
4. **Warranty terms matter** - NDL vs. limited, workmanship duration
5. **Safety records indicate management quality** - WSIB ratings vary from 0.72 to 1.12
6. **Value engineering shows expertise** - Heritage demonstrates deeper thinking
7. **Phasing capability is critical** - This is an occupied pharmaceutical facility
8. **Allowances vs. inclusions** - Some bidders include, some exclude, some use allowances

## Facilitator Notes

### Common Participant Reactions

1. **"Metro is obviously the worst"** - Some participants will immediately recognize the 25+ exclusions as a red flag. Ask them to quantify the potential change order exposure.

2. **"Lakeside must have misread the RFP"** - Participants may give Lakeside the benefit of the doubt. Point out that even if unintentional, the non-compliances are material.

3. **"Summit is the safe choice"** - Some participants will gravitate toward the premium option. Ask them to justify the 59% premium to ownership.

4. **"Heritage's VE options are confusing"** - Some participants may find the VE analysis overwhelming. This is actually a sign of sophistication - the bidder is thinking critically about the project.

### Key Discussion Moments

1. **When participants discover Lakeside's non-compliances:** Ask "Is this honest mistake or intentional? Does it matter?"

2. **When participants count Metro's exclusions:** Ask them to estimate the total change order exposure.

3. **When comparing Pinnacle vs. Heritage:** Ask "What matters more - lower price or pharmaceutical experience?"

4. **When discussing Summit:** Ask "When is the premium option the right choice?"

### Time Management

- If running short on time, focus on the compliance matrix and top three bidders (Pinnacle, Heritage, Summit)
- If participants finish early, have them draft negotiation points for the recommended bidder
- The change order exposure calculation for Metro is a good extension exercise
