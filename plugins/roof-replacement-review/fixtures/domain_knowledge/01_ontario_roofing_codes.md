# Ontario Roofing Code Reference for Tender Evaluation

**Purpose.** Authoritative reference for an AI evaluator reviewing roofing contractor tender submissions in Ontario. Every rule below is tied to a regulatory citation or recognized industry standard. When the evaluator flags a compliance concern, it should cite the specific clause listed here rather than relying on model memory.

**Scope.** Ontario Building Code (O. Reg. 332/12 and 2024 update), Occupational Health and Safety Act regulations (O. Reg. 213/91 Construction Projects, O. Reg. 297/13 Working at Heights, O. Reg. 278/05 Asbestos, Reg. 833 Control of Exposure), and referenced CSA/ULC/FM standards. Currency verified to 2022 or later where practical; OBC is the 2012 code as amended through the 2024 Compendium.

**Caveat.** Where a specific clause number is cited, the evaluator should quote it verbatim. Where only the section is cited (e.g., "OBC 9.26"), the evaluator should treat this as a topical pointer, not a precise clause reference.

---

## 1. Commercial / Low-Slope (OBC Part 3)

Part 3 governs buildings greater than 3 storeys in building height or greater than 600 m² in building area (OBC 1.1.2.2). Roofing work on these buildings must also comply with Part 5 (Environmental Separation) for air, vapour, and water control.

### 1.1 Fire Classification — CAN/ULC-S107

**Citation.** OBC 3.1.15.2 and OBC 9.26.2.1; test standard CAN/ULC-S107 "Standard Method of Fire Tests of Roof Coverings."

**Rule.** Every roof covering must carry a Class A, B, or C rating per CAN/ULC-S107. The specific class required depends on occupancy, construction type, and proximity to property lines per OBC Part 3. Test criteria: flame spread must not exceed ~10 ft in the first 10 min and ~14 ft over the full test.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- Named roof assembly (membrane, insulation, cover board, deck) with a documented CAN/ULC-S107 listing (UL/ULC, FM Approvals, or ICC-ES report) specifying Class A, B, or C.
- Manufacturer listing matches the as-proposed assembly (membrane type, attachment method, insulation thickness, cover board, deck).

**Red flags.**
- "Class A rated materials" stated without a specific assembly listing.
- Listing for a different attachment method than proposed (e.g., adhered listing used for a mechanically fastened bid).
- No ULC/UL listing number cited.

### 1.2 Wind Uplift — CSA A123.21 (with FM 1-29 as a design aid)

**Citation.** National Building Code of Canada (adopted by OBC) references CSA A123.21 "Standard test method for the dynamic wind uplift resistance of membrane-roofing systems" (current edition CSA A123.21-20). CSA A123.21 has been the sole Canadian standard for flat-roof wind uplift since ~2015. FM Global Data Sheet 1-29 ("Roof Deck Securement and Above-Deck Roof Components") and FM 1-28 ("Wind Design") are widely accepted design references in Canadian practice.

**Rule.**
- Wind loads are calculated per NBC Part 4 (applicable through OBC) using the reference velocity pressure (q) for the project location.
- Assembly uplift rating must meet or exceed factored uplift pressures for each zone (Zone 1 field, Zone 1' interior field, Zone 2 perimeter, Zone 3 corner) — CSA A123.21 uses zones consistent with FM 1-29.
- Enhancement factors for Zones 2 and 3: double field-zone fastener density at perimeter; doubled again at corners is common prescriptive practice (FM 1-29 §2.2.3.4).
- NRC's Wind-RCI calculator is the recognized design tool.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- Wind load calculation for the specific project (location, building height, exposure) with q value and factored uplift pressure per zone.
- Named CSA A123.21 listing (or FM 1-60/1-90/1-120 equivalent) for the complete assembly as installed.
- Fastening schedule showing field, perimeter, and corner patterns with fastener type, length, and spacing.

**Red flags.**
- "Wind-rated system" with no calculation and no listed assembly.
- Uniform field-zone fastening used at corners and perimeter.
- Reliance on legacy FM 1-90 static testing only (CSA A123.21 dynamic test is the Canadian standard).

### 1.3 Insulation, Vapour Barrier, and Thermal Continuity — OBC SB-10 / SB-12

**Citation.** OBC Supplementary Standard SB-10 (energy efficiency, applies to Part 3 buildings and large Part 9 buildings covered by ASHRAE 90.1 adoption); OBC SB-12 (housing and small buildings). Part 5 (5.3–5.5) governs air barrier, vapour barrier, and thermal continuity.

**Climate zones (ASHRAE / NECB).** Most of southern Ontario (GTA, Niagara, Hamilton, Windsor, London) is **Zone 5**. Ottawa, much of eastern/central Ontario, and northern regions are **Zone 6** (and Zone 7A further north).

**Rule (prescriptive R-values, above-deck insulation, low-slope roofs).** Under SB-10 (tracking ASHRAE 90.1 / NECB 2017 as amended):
- **Zone 5:** minimum ~R-30ci (continuous above deck) — typically specified as ~R-35 effective to meet NECB assembly targets.
- **Zone 6:** minimum ~R-35ci continuous — typically ~R-40 effective.
- Values vary by compliance path (prescriptive vs. performance) and by edition; evaluator must check the path cited in the bid against the current SB-10 edition.

**Continuity and thermal bridging.** Part 5 requires continuity of the thermal, air, and vapour control layers. Fasteners, parapets, curbs, and penetrations are thermal bridges that must be accounted for in effective R-value (NECB 3.2.1). Two layers of insulation with staggered joints is standard practice to maintain continuity.

**Vapour barrier.** OBC 5.5.1 requires a vapour barrier on the warm side of the assembly sufficient to limit moisture migration. On conventional low-slope roofs, a bituminous or self-adhered vapour retarder over the deck is typical; perm rating must be appropriate to interior humidity class.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- R-value calculation meeting SB-10 for the project's climate zone, with the compliance path named (prescriptive / trade-off / performance).
- Insulation layer count and joint stagger.
- Vapour barrier product, perm rating, and location in assembly.
- Parapet, curb, and penetration detailing that maintains thermal/air/vapour continuity.

**Red flags.**
- Single layer of rigid insulation on a commercial roof (joint leakage and thermal bridging).
- R-value stated without climate zone or SB-10 path.
- No mention of vapour barrier, or vapour barrier on the cold side.
- No detail for parapet/curb thermal break.

### 1.4 Drainage, Slope, and Overflow

**Citation.** OBC 3.2.4 (drainage), OBC 5.6 (precipitation), Ontario Plumbing Code (OBC Part 7) for roof drains, CRCA Roofing Specifications Manual (industry practice).

**Rule.**
- Minimum positive slope to drains is **2% (1:50, or approximately 1/4 inch per foot)** per CRCA and NRC best practice. OBC requires "slope to drain" without a universal numeric minimum; 2% is the accepted industry floor.
- Primary drains must be supplemented by **overflow drainage** (scuppers or secondary drains) set at a height that will not exceed the roof's structural loading capacity if primary drains clog.
- Overflow scuppers: typically set 2 inches (50 mm) above finished roof surface; sized per the Plumbing Code.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- Slope achieved via tapered insulation design with a layout drawing showing crickets at curbs and saddles between drains.
- Primary drain count, size, and location.
- Overflow scupper or secondary drain design with invert height.

**Red flags.**
- "Dead-flat" roof with no tapered insulation.
- No overflow provision on a parapeted roof.
- Crickets omitted at large penetrations (skylights, rooftop units) on the uphill side.

### 1.5 Parapet Flashing

**Citation.** OBC 5.6 (precipitation control); CRCA Roofing Specifications Manual Section on flashings; NRC *Canadian Roofing Contractors Handbook*.

**Rule.**
- Base flashing must extend a minimum of 8 inches (200 mm) up the parapet from the finished roof surface.
- Counterflashing (cap flashing or through-wall flashing) must overlap base flashing by at least 4 inches (100 mm) and be mechanically terminated.
- Parapet caps must have positive slope to the roof side and drip edges on both faces.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- Parapet detail drawing showing base flashing height, counterflashing overlap, termination bar, sealant, and cap.

**Red flags.**
- Base flashing less than 200 mm.
- Caulk-only counterflashing termination with no mechanical attachment.
- Flat parapet cap with no slope or drip.

### 1.6 Roof Access and Fall Protection Anchors — CSA Z91 and OBC 3.4.4 / 3.8

**Citation.** OBC 3.4.4 (roof access). Permanent anchor systems for window cleaning / façade maintenance per OBC where roof is ≥ 8 m above adjacent grade: CSA Z91 "Health and Safety Code for Suspended Equipment Operations." Construction fall protection: O. Reg. 213/91 Part III. Inspection: CAN/CSA-Z271 (inspection of roof anchors and suspended platforms).

**Rule.**
- Permanent roof anchors: designed, installed, and tested per CSA Z91; engineer-sealed layout; re-inspection not less than every 12 months (or manufacturer's stated interval, whichever is shorter).
- Construction fall protection is separately triggered any time a worker may fall 3 m or more (or into operating machinery or liquid) — O. Reg. 213/91 §26.1.
- Edge protection (guardrails) or a control zone is required whenever a worker could fall from a roof edge — see §4.2 below.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- For permanent-anchor scope: engineer-sealed anchor layout, CSA Z91 compliance, and annual inspection plan.
- For construction fall protection: site-specific fall protection plan naming anchor points, lifelines, connectors, and rescue plan.

**Red flags.**
- No rescue plan (required under §26.1 for any fall-arrest system).
- Anchors specified by product but not by engineered layout or load path.
- No annual re-inspection provision on a tender involving permanent anchors.

### 1.7 Smoke/Heat Vents and Fire Stopping at Penetrations

**Citation.** OBC 3.2.5 (vertical openings / firestopping); smoke vents per OBC 3.2.8 (high-challenge fire occupancies) and NFPA 204 where referenced; CAN/ULC-S115 "Fire Tests of Firestop Systems."

**Rule.**
- All roof penetrations through rated assemblies must be firestopped with a CAN/ULC-S115 listed system matching the penetrant, substrate, and rating.
- Smoke and heat vents, where required by OBC Part 3 for the occupancy, must have listed activation devices and be integrated with the roof assembly without compromising membrane or fire rating.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- For each penetration type (pipe, duct, conduit, drain), the CAN/ULC-S115 listed system number.
- For smoke vents: make/model, listing, activation type, curb detail.

**Red flags.**
- "Firestop as required" boilerplate with no listed systems.
- Generic expanding foam or silicone used in rated assemblies.

## 2. Residential / Steep-Slope (OBC Part 9)

Part 9 governs houses and small buildings not exceeding 3 storeys or 600 m². Roofing provisions are concentrated in OBC Section 9.26 (Roofing), 9.19 (Roof Spaces), and 9.23 (Wood-Frame Construction).

### 2.1 Minimum Slope by Material

**Citation.** OBC 9.26 and referenced CSA/ULC material standards.

**Rule (minimum slopes, rise:run).**
- **Asphalt shingles:** minimum **1:3** (4:12) for standard application; lower slopes (down to 1:6 / 2:12) permitted with supplemental underlayment per CSA A123.51 and manufacturer instructions.
- **Metal roofing (lapped/standing seam):** typically minimum **1:4** (3:12) for standing seam with sealed seams; 1:3 for lap-seam. Verify against manufacturer listing.
- **Wood shingles/shakes:** minimum **1:3** (4:12).
- **Built-up and membrane (on Part 9 buildings):** minimum positive slope; industry minimum 2% (1:50) to drain.

**Tender must demonstrate.** Proposed material matches actual roof slope; manufacturer installation instructions for the slope range are cited.

**Red flags.** Standard asphalt shingles on a 2:12 roof without supplemental ice-and-water shield full-coverage and specified low-slope shingle per manufacturer.

### 2.2 Eave Protection (Ice & Water Shield) — OBC 9.26.5

**Citation.** OBC **9.26.5.1** (note: commonly cited as "9.26.6" in older references, but current numbering is 9.26.5).

**Rule.** Eave protection on shingle, shake, and tile roofs extending from the edge of the roof a minimum of **900 mm up the slope** to a line **not less than 300 mm inside the inner face of the exterior wall**.

**Exceptions (eave protection not required):**
- Over unheated garages, carports, porches.
- Where the roof overhang exceeds 900 mm along the slope.
- On asphalt shingle roofs installed per specific CSA A123.51 provisions (self-sealing shingles on certain slopes).
- Roofs with slopes of 1:1.5 or steeper.
- In regions with 3,500 or fewer heating degree-days (most of Ontario exceeds this, so this exception rarely applies).

**Tender must demonstrate.** Self-adhered ice-and-water membrane product named; coverage extent at eaves, valleys, skylights, and around penetrations; minimum 900 mm measured and line at least 300 mm inside exterior wall face.

**Red flags.** Standard felt underlayment used at eaves. No ice-and-water in valleys or around skylights. 600 mm coverage "to match existing."

### 2.3 Underlayment

**Citation.** OBC 9.26.4; CSA A123.3 (asphalt-saturated felt) or CSA A220 (synthetic underlayments).

**Rule.** Minimum #15 felt (or listed synthetic equivalent) over the entire roof deck for asphalt shingles, installed with headlaps per manufacturer (minimum 50 mm headlap, 100 mm side lap typical). Low-slope applications (1:6 to 1:3) require double-layer or specified self-adhered underlayment.

**Tender must demonstrate.** Underlayment product, weight/type, lap schedule.

**Red flags.** Shingles directly on deck. Underlayment omitted between ice-and-water zone and ridge.

### 2.4 Ventilation — OBC 9.19.1.2

**Citation.** OBC **9.19.1.2**.

**Rule.**
- Baseline: unobstructed vent area **not less than 1/300 of the insulated ceiling area**.
- Low-slope or joist-constructed roofs (slope less than 1:6): **1/150** of insulated ceiling area.
- **Balance:** at least 25% of openings at the top of the space and at least 25% at the bottom.
- Vents must conform to CAN3-A93-M "Natural Airflow Ventilators."

**Tender must demonstrate.** Calculated net free vent area (NFVA); intake (soffit) and exhaust (ridge/roof/gable) breakdown with 25/25 minimum confirmed; no mixing of powered exhaust with passive ridge vents (short-circuits flow).

**Red flags.** Ridge vent added without soffit intake. Bathroom/kitchen exhaust dumped into attic. Ratio calculation omitted.

### 2.5 Asphalt Shingle Fastening

**Citation.** OBC 9.26.7; CSA A123.51 "Asphalt Shingle Application on Roof Slopes 1:3 and Greater"; manufacturer installation instructions (which are binding under OBC 9.26.2).

**Rule.**
- **Nails (standard):** galvanized steel, head diameter ≥ 9.5 mm, shank ≥ 2.95 mm (11 ga), length sufficient to penetrate deck fully or at least 12 mm into solid lumber.
- **Fastener count:** **4 nails per shingle** (standard); **6 nails per shingle** in high-wind zones (generally coastal, elevated, or exposed sites, and as required by manufacturer for warranty).
- Nails placed ≤ 25 mm from each side edge and ≤ 25 mm above the self-seal strip line (never in or above the sealant).
- Staples are generally prohibited by manufacturers and should not be accepted.

**Tender must demonstrate.** Nail spec, count per shingle, location relative to sealant strip; high-wind upgrade where site is exposed.

**Red flags.** Staples. 3-nail pattern. Overdriven or shiner nails. "Per manufacturer" with no product named.

### 2.6 Drip Edge and Starter Strip

**Citation.** OBC 9.26.4.2 (flashing at eaves and gables); CSA A123.51 §5 (starter course).

**Rule.**
- Drip edge (metal flashing) at eaves and rakes, minimum 50 mm onto the roof deck and extending past the fascia to direct water away.
- At eaves: drip edge **under** the ice-and-water membrane. At rakes: drip edge **over** the underlayment.
- Starter strip: purpose-made starter (or field shingle with tabs cut off and reversed) along the eave with sealant aligned to seal the first course.

**Tender must demonstrate.** Drip edge gauge, material (aluminum, galvanized), and install sequence. Starter strip product named.

**Red flags.** Drip edge omitted. Drip edge installed over ice-and-water at eaves (reverses water path). Starter strip omitted and first course relied on for seal.

## 3. Working at Heights / OHSA

### 3.1 Working at Heights Training — O. Reg. 297/13

**Citation.** O. Reg. 297/13 "Occupational Health and Safety Awareness and Training," §6–7.

**Rule.** Every worker on a construction project who may use any specified method of fall protection (travel restraint, fall restriction, fall arrest, safety net, work belt, safety belt) must complete a **CPO-approved Working at Heights training program before working at heights**. Validity: **3 years** from completion; renewal via a CPO-approved refresher (typically 4 hours vs. the initial ~8-hour program). Mandatory since April 1, 2015 (full transition April 1, 2017).

**Tender must demonstrate.** Named CPO-approved training provider, certificate numbers, and expiry dates for all workers who will be on-roof. Plan for refresher renewals during the contract term.

**Red flags.** No training records. Expired certificates. Non-CPO-approved training (e.g., out-of-province cards not recognized in Ontario).

### 3.2 Notice of Project (NOP)

**Citation.** O. Reg. 213/91 §6, filed via Ontario Ministry of Labour Immigration Training and Skills Development (MLITSD) eNOP portal.

**Rule.** NOP required where total project cost ≥ **$50,000** (labour + materials) OR for specific hazard triggers including **work on a sloped or built-up roof where workers may work within 2 m of the edge or a roof opening (skylight)**. NOP must be filed before work begins. (A proposal to replace the $50k trigger with an expanded hazard list is under consultation but not in force as of 2026.)

**Tender must demonstrate.** NOP filing commitment with filing date, project number, and constructor identified.

**Red flags.** No mention of NOP on a project that clearly triggers one. Constructor unclear.

### 3.3 Fall Arrest vs. Fall Restraint — O. Reg. 213/91 §§26.1–26.9

**Citation.** O. Reg. 213/91 Part III Sections 26–26.9.

**Rule (hierarchy of controls, in order of preference).**
1. **Guardrail** (preferred — passive, no PPE required).
2. **Travel restraint** (worker cannot physically reach the edge — anchor + lanyard sized so the fall is impossible).
3. **Fall restricting system** (limits free-fall distance to ≤ 0.6 m).
4. **Fall arrest system** (full-body harness + shock-absorbing lanyard + anchor rated ≥ 22.2 kN static; clearance calculation required).
5. **Safety net** (last resort).

**Trigger:** fall of **3 m or more**, or fall into operating machinery, water, or hazardous substance.

Anchors must be rated ≥ 22.2 kN (5,000 lbf) ultimate for fall arrest, ≥ 3.5 kN for restraint. Engineered roof anchors are required where no suitable structural member exists.

**Tender must demonstrate.** Site-specific fall protection plan identifying which method is used in which zone; anchor specifications; harness/lanyard/SRL inventory; **rescue plan** (mandatory per §26.1).

**Red flags.** Default to fall arrest without considering guardrails or restraint (violates hierarchy). No rescue plan. Anchors un-engineered. Lanyards used where clearance is insufficient.

### 3.4 Guardrails — O. Reg. 213/91 §26.3

**Citation.** O. Reg. 213/91 §26.3.

**Rule (guardrail specifications).**
- **Top rail:** 0.9 to 1.1 m above work surface.
- **Intermediate rail:** midway between top rail and toe board.
- **Toe board:** minimum 89 mm high.
- **Loads:** 675 N point load laterally on top rail; 450 N downward on top rail; 450 N on intermediate rail; 225 N lateral on toe board.
- **Wood option:** S-P-F construction-grade or better; posts minimum 38 × 89 mm, spacing ≤ 2.4 m; top and intermediate rails minimum 38 × 89 mm.
- **Built-up roof exception (§26.3(6)):** a barrier shall be placed at least **2 m from the perimeter** in the immediate work area on a built-up roof under construction/repair (control-zone alternative to full guardrail).

**Tender must demonstrate.** Guardrail/barrier layout; materials; or control-zone demarcation on built-up roof work.

**Red flags.** 2x4 nailed to parapet calling itself a guardrail without load verification. No toe board. Missing mid-rail. Control zone without clear demarcation (warning line + signage).

## 4. Waste and Environmental

### 4.1 Asphalt Shingle Disposal

**Citation.** Ontario Environmental Protection Act; Resource Recovery and Circular Economy Act, 2016; municipal bylaws.

**Rule.** Asphalt shingles are not a banned waste in Ontario, but landfill tipping is discouraged. Recycled asphalt shingles (RAS) can be processed into hot-mix asphalt aggregate, road base, or fuel. Canada generates roughly 1.25 million tonnes of discarded asphalt roofing annually. Asphalt shingles contain trace PAHs; commingled disposal is acceptable at most Ontario MSW facilities but C&D-specific recyclers are increasingly available (e.g., GFL, Waste Connections, regional C&D processors).

**Tender must demonstrate.** Named waste disposal / recycling facility; diversion rate target; bin type (roll-off), load weights tracked.

**Red flags.** "Dispose of debris" boilerplate with no facility named. No diversion commitment on a project with sustainability targets.

### 4.2 Asbestos in Pre-1980 Roofs — O. Reg. 278/05

**Citation.** O. Reg. 278/05 "Designated Substance — Asbestos on Construction Projects and in Buildings and Repair Operations," read with OHSA §30 (Designated Substance Report).

**Rule.**
- OHSA §30: **Owner must determine if a designated substance is present at the project and provide a list (including asbestos) to all prospective contractors before tender.** This is the **Designated Substance Report (DSR)** — the evaluator should verify the owner has provided one and that the contractor has received and acknowledged it.
- Asbestos-containing material (ACM): bulk sample ≥ **0.5%** asbestos by dry weight per EPA/600/R-93/116.
- Buildings constructed before ~1990 may contain ACM in built-up roofing (asphalt felts, roof mastics, flashing cement), pipe insulation at penetrations, and transite vent piping.
- Work on ACM is classified Type 1 / Type 2 / Type 3, each with specific control measures, training, and notification requirements.
- **Notification to MLITSD** is required before Type 3 asbestos operations commence.

**Tender must demonstrate.**
- Confirmation that DSR (or, if allowed, a survey confirming no ACM) has been received.
- If ACM is present: classification (Type 1/2/3), control plan, trained personnel (Asbestos Abatement Worker / Supervisor training per O. Reg. 278/05 §10–11), notification to MLITSD for Type 3.

**Red flags.** Pre-1980 building with no asbestos survey referenced. "Assume no asbestos" on a 1960s built-up roof. Abatement priced but no Type classification.

### 4.3 Silica Exposure — Reg. 833 and Ontario Silica Control Tool

**Citation.** O. Reg. 833 (Control of Exposure to Biological or Chemical Agents) — construction projects are regulated under Reg. 833, not O. Reg. 490/09, which explicitly exempts construction. IHSA Ontario Silica Control Tool is the recognized compliance tool.

**Rule.**
- Occupational Exposure Limit (OEL) for **respirable crystalline silica: 0.05 mg/m³** (8-hour TWA).
- Silica exposures on roofing arise from cutting concrete, masonry, or cementitious decks (anchor installation, curb cutting, core drilling). Dust suppression (wet cutting, LEV) is required where exposures may exceed the OEL.
- IHSA Silica Control Tool classifies tasks and specifies prescribed controls (Type 1 / Type 2 / Type 3 operations parallel to asbestos classification).

**Tender must demonstrate.** Named silica-generating tasks; control method (wet cutting, on-tool LEV, enclosure); respiratory protection where residual exposure remains above OEL; worker training.

**Red flags.** Dry cutting of concrete deck with no dust control. No silica exposure assessment. Respirators specified without fit-test or training records.
